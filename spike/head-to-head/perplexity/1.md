## Where the wording changed

1. The year changed while the number stayed fixed.  
   In the reported February 2012 archive, eight seconds describes humans in 2012. In the March 2015 version, the same eight seconds describes 2013. No newly identifiable study accompanies that change. Microsoft then repeats the 2013 version. [tryhugo](https://www.tryhugo.app/blog/average-attention-span-goldfish-myth)

2. A statistics table became an authoritative statement of decline.  
   Microsoft placed the three figures beneath “We know human attention is dwindling.” It retained the Statistic Brain credit, but packaging them inside a report containing original survey and brain-activity research made it easy to confuse the borrowed table with the report’s actual findings. [bbc](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790)

3. A borrowed figure became something “Microsoft found.”  
   TIME explicitly attributed the eight-second result to a “new study” and wrote “Microsoft found” the decline. That wording removes the distinction between Microsoft’s research and the external Statistic Brain figures. [time](https://time.com/3858309/attention-spans-goldfish/)

4. A dated assertion became a timeless—or current—human limit.  
   Your wording, “The average human attention span is 8 seconds,” drops the original year. TIME similarly used “now” and translated the table into people generally “los[ing] concentration after eight seconds.” That is stronger than merely reporting a dated statistic, and it is not justified by the cited chain. [tryhugo](https://www.tryhugo.app/blog/average-attention-span-goldfish-myth)

## How many independent sources actually stand behind it?

The distinction is between a publisher of numbers and evidence for those numbers:

- One identifiable originating web source: Statistic Brain, with the comparison located by February 2012, subject to the archive-verification limitation above. [tryhugo](https://www.tryhugo.app/blog/average-attention-span-goldfish-myth)
- No verified independent studies supporting the eight-second average, the nine-second goldfish average, or the comparison between them. The named upstream organizations could not substantiate it, and the identifiable web-use paper does not establish it. [bbc](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790)
- Microsoft, TIME, and other outlets are repetitions or amplifiers—not independent confirmations. Microsoft’s own 2,000-person survey and 112-person brain-activity study do not turn the externally sourced table into a measured result. [bbc](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790)

The precise conclusion is therefore not “Microsoft discovered that humans have an eight-second attention span.” It is: an unsubstantiated Statistic Brain table was incorporated into a Microsoft marketing report, then reported as a Microsoft scientific finding. Its ultimate author and evidentiary origin remain unestablished. [bbc](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790)
