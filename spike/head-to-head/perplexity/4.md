But its ingredients are older: a 2009 Google estimate, a February 2023 model of ChatGPT’s computing costs, and an Alphabet executive’s statement that an AI exchange could cost ten times more—in money, not explicitly electricity. ## Where the wording—and the claim—changed

### 1. Financial cost became electricity consumption

Reuters’s February 2023 report says:

> “Having an exchange with AI known as a large language model likely cost 10 times more than a standard keyword search.” [reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/tech-giants-ai-like-bing-bard-poses-billion-dollar-search-problem-2023-02-22/)

In the *Joule* commentary, de Vries quotes that statement and immediately continues:

> “As a standard Google search reportedly uses 0.3 Wh of electricity, this suggests an electricity consumption of approximately 3 Wh per LLM interaction.” [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)

That is the first consequential inferential change I could locate. A ratio of financial costs does not by itself establish the same ratio of electricity use: the Reuters article also discusses hardware expenditure and depreciation, not just electricity. [reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/tech-giants-ai-like-bing-bard-poses-billion-dollar-search-problem-2023-02-22/)

However, it would be incomplete to say the entire claim rests only on that conversion. De Vries also supplied a separate hardware-based calculation that produced a similar number. [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)

### 2. A cost model became an electricity-per-request estimate

SemiAnalysis explicitly calls its analysis a “cost model” and asks readers to challenge its assumptions. Among those assumptions were:

- A dense 175-billion-parameter model.
- Approximately 2,000 tokens per response.
- Thirteen million daily active users.
- Fifteen responses per user.
- About 3,617 HGX A100 servers.
- Assumptions about hardware utilization, idle time and GPU-hour pricing. [newsletter.semianalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-inference-cost-of-search-disruption)

De Vries translates the server estimate into approximately 564 MWh per day, then divides by an estimated 195 million daily requests to obtain about 2.9 Wh per request. He describes the result as “at most,” not as a directly observed average across actual ChatGPT requests. [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)

This was an explicit estimation exercise—not a disclosure of ChatGPT’s electricity meter readings.

### 3. “At most” became an unqualified per-request figure

The shift through the main chain is particularly clear:

| Stage | Wording |
|---|---|
| De Vries, October 2023 | “At most, 2.9 Wh per request.”  [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf) |
| IEA, January 2024 | “ChatGPT (2.9 Wh per request),” alongside the average Google-search figure.  [howaiworks](https://howaiworks.ai/glossary/ai-energy-consumption/) |
| Goldman Sachs, May 2024 | “A single ChatGPT query requires 2.9 watt-hours”; opening sentence calls the comparison an average.  [goldmansachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/AI-poised-to-drive-160-increase-in-power-demand) |
| Later repetition | “Every ChatGPT Query Uses 10x More Energy.”  [hiwavemakers](https://hiwavemakers.com/blog/hidden-carbon-cost-ai-every-chatgpt-query-green-it-kids/) |

The numerical precision survived while the upper-bound language, model assumptions and historical context largely disappeared.

### 4. Two different vintages became a timeless comparison

The denominator describes Google’s estimate in January 2009. The numerator models an early-2023 ChatGPT deployment. The comparison is therefore not a contemporary benchmark of the two services under matched conditions. [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)

Its accounting boundaries also warrant caution: Google explicitly included indexing work in its number, while de Vries’s ChatGPT calculation was based on inference servers and request volume, not an equivalent complete lifecycle accounting. [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)

### 5. “Nearly ten” became exactly ten

The familiar figures imply approximately \(2.9/0.3 \approx 9.7\), so “nearly ten times” is a reasonable rounding of those inputs. The important distortion is not the rounding—it is treating the inputs as universal, measured constants. [asociace](https://asociace.ai/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ai-spotreba.pdf)
