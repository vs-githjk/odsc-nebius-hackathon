## Full answer (pasted verbatim)
**The claim traces to a sentence in Wikipedia's "Water speed record" article, and I found no source for the 85%.** The sourced counts are about half: 7 of 14 in a 2001 book review and 7 of 13 in 2024. The "85% since 1940" is a distortion that was never tied to any citation I could find.

## Where the wording changed

- **2001, the count:** 7 of 14, about 50%. The review covers contestants from 1928 to 1978 and has no "since 1940".
- **Early Wikipedia, "50%":** My inference is that this rounded the 7-of-14 figure. The 50% copies cite Wikipedia and are consistent with a 2006–07 date, but I couldn't retrieve Wikipedia's edit history.
- **By about 2008–10, "85% since 1940":** The percentage jumps and a start date appears, with no new source. I can't tell who made the edit or why.
- **Retellings add emphasis.** Boat Lovers Direct adds "perhaps" and "professional." Atlas Obscura recasts it as "mortality rate among water speed competitors." The claim you gave also calls the record a "competition," which it isn't.
- **2023–24, back to counts:** 5 of 7 since 1940, then 7 of 13 since June 1930.

## How many independent sources stand behind it?

**None for 85%.**

- Every version traces to one unsourced Wikipedia sentence, and nothing I found independently supports it.
- It conflicts with the same article's own history. That history names fatal attempts by Cobb (1952), Verga (1954), Campbell (1967), Taylor (1980) and Arfons (1989), five since 1940. The same article also names several survivors since 1940, including Sayres, Ted Jones, Campbell's earlier record runs and Warby. That is at least nine people, so the rate is at most about 56%. The 85% figure doesn't fit that list.
- For "roughly half," I found two sources: Harris and Rimmer via Motor Sport (2001) and Guinness (2024). Both are compilations of the same history, so I wouldn't count them as independent.
- I couldn't reconcile which seven deaths make up the "7 of 13." My guess is Segrave's co-driver is counted alongside the six pilots, but that is unconfirmed.

I couldn't open Wikipedia's revision history, the Guinness article, the Harris book or Leslie Field's page. The undated pages are placed by internal clues, so treat their order as approximate.
