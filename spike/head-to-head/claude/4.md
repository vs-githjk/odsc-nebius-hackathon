## Full answer (pasted verbatim)
**The "ten times" comes from a February 2023 remark by Alphabet's chairman about *cost*, which Alex de Vries converted into electricity in 2023.** The International Energy Agency (IEA) then repeated it as a measured comparison. The ratio was an input to the 3 Wh figure, not an independent result, and newer estimates put a ChatGPT query near 0.3 Wh.

## Where the wording changed

- **2009:** Google stated 0.0003 kWh per search. This was a rebuttal to a claim that a search produced about seven grams of CO2.
- **Feb. 2023, from cost to electricity:** Hennessy's remark was about expense. The Reuters piece is about dollar costs, such as a fifth of a cent per search at Morgan Stanley's estimate. He didn't say electricity.
- **Oct. 2023, the key step:** de Vries took Hennessy's "10 times more" and Google's 0.3 Wh and wrote that this suggests approximately 3 Wh per LLM interaction. He then said it aligns with SemiAnalysis' figure of at most 2.9 Wh per request. The 10× was therefore an input to the 3 Wh, and "at most" disappeared in later retellings.
- **Jan. 2024, IEA:** The two numbers appear as a plain comparison, with no mention of Hennessy or the "at most" qualifier.
- **2024:** A UN page says ChatGPT consumes 10 times the electricity of a Google search, "reported the International Energy Agency."
- **2025:** Epoch calls it a commonly-cited claim and replaces it with 0.3 Wh.
- **2026:** One source says some articles now keep the "10×" headline by lowering Google's figure to 0.03–0.04 Wh. I couldn't verify that.

## How many independent sources stand behind it?

**None that independently confirm a 10× ratio.**

- **The ChatGPT side** rests on one analysis (de Vries). It uses two loosely independent inputs: a cost remark and SemiAnalysis's model of chip counts and full-load power. Epoch noted its author assumed older, less efficient chips. de Vries also cites a measured 3.96 Wh per request for the open BLOOM model, but that is a different model.
- **The Google side** is a single self-reported 2009 figure. It came from Google itself, not an outside measurement.
- **The IEA, EPRI, the UN and the press** are downstream of de Vries.
- **Newer estimates cut against it.** Epoch (independent), OpenAI (undisclosed method) and Google (published method, for Gemini) all land around 0.24–0.34 Wh per query. Against Google's old 0.3 Wh that is roughly parity, though Google hasn't published a current per-search figure for the comparison.

I couldn't open the Reuters original, the IEA report, SemiAnalysis, Google's blog or EPRI's paper. The 2026 dates come from search-index ages.
