Where the wording changed
Paper to press release: 7.78 points on a secondary outcome became “about eight points” and “nearly eight points.” The release kept the one-third dropout caveat but dropped three things. It did not say IQ was a secondary outcome, that only 103 of 206 children were tested, or that the result weakened in a sensitivity analysis. In that analysis, which excluded 20 women who got open-label supplement during COVID, the IQ confidence interval widened to include no effect.

Press release to StudyFinds: “scored higher” became “raised,” and “jump” appeared in the headline, both causal-sounding. The article itself does carry the caveats.
StudyFinds to your claim: The wording “choline supplements” blurs the specific intervention. The trial tested very high-dose phosphatidylcholine, not an ordinary prenatal choline supplement. The claim also drops the single-trial, secondary-outcome and attrition caveats.
How many independent sources stand behind it
One trial, one research group, and no independent replication of the IQ result.
