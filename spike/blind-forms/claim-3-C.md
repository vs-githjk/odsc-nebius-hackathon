# Form C: claim 3

Claim: "Communication is 7% words, 38% tone of voice and 55% body language."

- Earliest URL: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0024648 (earliest verified appearance of all three exact percentages together); https://doi.org/10.1037/h0024532 (earliest original experiment in the main lineage)
- Its date: June 1967; May 1967
- Stop statement: "Final count: two original studies, one originating research group, and zero verified independent studies establishing the claimed 7–38–55 split for communication in general."

## Chain, newest to oldest

1. https://law.temple.edu/aer/2021/05/03/brain-lessons-the-seven-percent-delusion/ (May 3, 2021)
   > "Documents the claim being used in legal advocacy training. The percentages are stated as general communication rules, which the author challenges."
2. https://www.ragan.com/debunking-3-myths-of-public-speaking/ (September 7, 2011)
   > "Reproduces the familiar words/tone/body-language formulation but explicitly rejects its applicability to normal conversations and presentations."
3. https://creativityworks.net/about/mehrabian-myth/ (Summer 2009)
   > "Animation challenging the assertion that words carry only 7% of what someone means."
4. https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/214121529.pdf (Summer 2007)
   > "Academic investigation documenting how the narrow experimental findings were generalized in communication textbooks and on the internet."
5. https://bookmarklee.co.uk/three-elements-of-communication-and-the-so-called-7-38-55-rule/ (January 7, 2007)
   > "Challenges the general rule and highlights that the original work was about attitudes."
6. https://dhemery.com/posts/2004/05/mehrabian/ (May 6, 2004)
   > "Explicitly documents the misconception and contrasts it with the original Total Liking equation."
7. https://linguistlist.org/issues/12/1332/ (May 15, 2001)
   > "Traces the percentages to the two 1967 experiments and identifies the exact passage where the three coefficients were proposed."
8. https://listser1000v.linguistlist.org/issues/12/12-1170.html (April 27, 2001)
   > "Documents a management course teaching the figures as percentages of message content."
9. Nonverbal Communication: Survey, Theory, and Research, Druckman, Rozelle and Baxter, pp. 84–85, no URL of its own (1982)
   > "Intermediate academic summary identified as a possible transmission point."
10. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED070130 (1971)
   > "Mehrabian's book explains his findings, including the original Total Liking formulation."
11. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0024648 (June 1967)
   > "Proposes the .07/.38/.55 coefficients for verbal, vocal and facial communication of attitudes."
12. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0024532 (May 1967)
   > "Examines how word meaning and tone affect interpretations of a speaker's attitude. Does not establish a general communication rule."
