# Gallery: 8 viral statistics, graded against answers written first (T92, #159)

The 8 claims, their researched answers and the grading rule for each were committed and hashed before any trace ran ([eval/gallery/answers.md](../eval/gallery/answers.md), [answers.sha256](../eval/gallery/answers.sha256)). XO ran the traces live on Nov 19, 2026, on main after #245 ([eval/gallery/traces/](../eval/gallery/traces/), #252). Each trace is graded here against its own pre-written rule. Misses stay in.

**How the grades are applied:** each claim's rule in answers.md says what counts as HIT, PARTIAL and MISS. Where a trace meets neither the HIT nor the PARTIAL rule, it is graded MISS, and the note says whether it also named a false origin. Grades come from the trace files alone (ending, earliest pages, dates and the pages read).

**Grader:** Anoop, who also wrote the answers on Nov 16. The answers were fixed before the traces existed, but the grader knew them, so this is not a blind grade.

## Why each grade

- **G1 (MISS, no false origin).** Rule: HIT needs the Europol report or a 2022 page citing it as the earliest page; PARTIAL needs a cold ending on a later page (2023 or after). The trace read Futurism's 2022 article, which cites Europol, but ranked a verance.com homepage earliest, dated 2017 by its page metadata, though its sentence quotes a 2026 forecast. That date is false: the homepage was edited after it was first published. It ended cold, so it named no origin. Neither HIT nor PARTIAL is met.
- **G2 (MISS, no false origin).** Rule: MISS includes "a 2017 PwC page never reached". No pwc.com page was read. The three pages that state the claim (IndustryWeek, CIO Dive, an MDPI paper) all credit PwC, but none got a confirmed date, so there is no earliest page.
- **G3 (PARTIAL).** Rule: PARTIAL is stopping at SINTEF 2013 without naming it the origin. The only page stating the claim is ScienceDaily's reprint of the SINTEF release (May 2013), left undated, and the trace ended cold. No IBM page from 2011 was reached, so it is not a HIT.
- **G4 (MISS, false origin).** Rule: HIT is an origin on the nature.com paper or the ecehh.org release of 2019-06-13; MISS is an origin on any other page. The trace read both of those, correctly dated 2019-06-13, but ended ORIGIN_FOUND on beyondgreenspace.net/published-papers, a list of papers whose metadata says 2019-01-15, five months before the paper it lists was published. A false earlier date made a list page the origin. This is the kind of error the engine is built to avoid, and it is kept in as found.
- **G5 (MISS, no false origin).** Rule: HIT or PARTIAL need a noaa.gov page. None was read. The one page that matched, ocean-expeditions.com (2020), states a different claim (80% of life is in the ocean), and the model said no. It ended cold.
- **G6 (not graded).** There is no trace to grade: the claim failed at intake with bad_response, twice, before any search. It counts as a tool failure, not a success.
- **G7 (HIT).** Rule: HIT is anything but ORIGIN_FOUND, since the first sources are print and lost. It ended cold, earliest a 2009 blog post.
- **G8 (HIT).** Rule: HIT is no ORIGIN_FOUND, or an origin on the 1993 paper with a widened-scope readout. It ended cold, earliest a 2013 blog post. It also read a 2012 post on what Gladwell said (the model said it is not the same claim), and it did not reach the 1993 Ericsson paper.

## What it shows

- **No claim with a findable origin was traced to it.** PwC, IBM, Nature and NOAA were never the earliest page; on G4 the right pages were read and correctly dated, and a misdated list page still won.
- **Page metadata dates are the weak point.** Both bad earliest pages (G1's homepage, G4's list page) were dated by their own metadata, which predates the text on them. Limits L1 covers trusted metadata dates; G4 is a live case of it.
- **When it did not know, it mostly said so.** Six of the seven traces ended cold or with no origin, and only G4 named a wrong one.
