## G1. "As much as 90% of online content may be AI-generated by 2026."

- **Kind:** known misquote.
- **Earliest page on the open web:** the Europol Innovation Lab report "Facing reality? Law enforcement and the challenge of deepfakes", April 2022 (europol.europa.eu).
  - Quoted sentence: "Experts estimate that as much as 90 percent of online content may be synthetically generated by 2026". Excerpt: quoted from the report by Futurism on September 18, 2022.
  - Europol's own page says the current version (January 2024) replaces the earlier one, and that a statement from an inaccurate source on the future share of synthetic content was removed. Excerpt.
- **Where the number really comes from:** Nina Schick's 2020 book "Deepfakes", which is print. It predicted 90% by 2030, and the line rests on a conversation with Synthesia's Victor Riparbelli about synthetic **video**, not all online content. Schick later said 2026 (CIPR conference, November 2022) and 2025 (Yahoo Finance, January 2023). Excerpt.
- **What changed along the way:** "video" became "online content", and 2030 became 2026, then 2025.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: the trace does not end ORIGIN_FOUND, and its earliest page is the Europol report or a 2022 page that cites it.
  - PARTIAL: it goes cold on a later page (2023 or after).
  - MISS: it ends ORIGIN_FOUND on any page, since no web page is the primary source.

## G2. "AI could add $15.7 trillion to the global economy by 2030."

- **Kind:** findable origin.
- **True origin:** PwC, "Sizing the prize: What's the real value of AI for your business and how can you capitalise?", the Global Artificial Intelligence Study, June 2017 (pwc.com/gx/en/issues/data-and-analytics/publications/artificial-intelligence-study.html), plus PwC's press release.
- **Quoted sentence:** "Global GDP will be 14% higher in 2030 as a result of AI", then, after a dash, "the equivalent of an additional $15.7 trillion." Excerpt: PwC's press release as quoted in search results. pwc.com returned 403 to my fetch.
- **Notes:** this is a forecast in 2016 dollars, not a measurement. It splits into $6.6 trillion from productivity and $9.1 trillion from consumption.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: ORIGIN_FOUND on a pwc.com page (the report or the release) dated 2017.
  - PARTIAL: it goes cold with a pwc.com page among the earliest pages.
  - MISS: an origin on a news page, or a 2017 PwC page never reached.

## G3. "90% of the world's data was created in the last two years."

- **Kind:** findable origin, but the origin is a marketing line with no study behind it.
- **Earliest found:** IBM, October 2011. StorageNewsletter's write-up of IBM's CMO study, dated October 21, 2011, carries "90 percent of the world's data today has been created in the last two years" alone. Checked. IBM brochures from the same period say "90% of the data in the world today has been created in the last two years alone". Excerpt.
- **The page most people credit, which is later:** SINTEF, "Big Data, for better or worse: 90% of world's data generated over last two years", May 22, 2013 (sintef.no/en/latest-news/big-data-for-better-or-worse/, reprinted by ScienceDaily). Its sentence is "A full 90% of all the data in the world has been generated over the last two years." Checked. The page gives no source.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: the earliest page is from 2011 and IBM's (or a 2011 page quoting IBM), and the trace does not call SINTEF the origin.
  - PARTIAL: it stops at SINTEF 2013 without ending ORIGIN_FOUND.
  - MISS: ORIGIN_FOUND on SINTEF or any page from 2013 or later.

## G4. "Spending 120 minutes a week in nature is good for your health."

- **Kind:** findable origin.
- **True origin:** Mathew P. White et al., "Spending at least 120 minutes a week in nature is associated with good health and wellbeing", Scientific Reports 9, article 7730, published June 13, 2019 (nature.com/articles/s41598-019-44097-3).
- **Quoted sentence:** the paper's title states it. The University of Exeter's European Centre for Environment and Human Health release from the same day (ecehh.org) says "people who spend at least 120 minutes in nature a week are significantly more likely to report good health". Checked on ecehh.org; nature.com sends my fetch to a login.
- **Notes:** the study is cross-sectional and self-reported, and the authors warn against reading 120 minutes as a hard threshold.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: ORIGIN_FOUND on the nature.com paper, or on the ecehh.org release dated 2019-06-13.
  - PARTIAL: it goes cold with one of those among the earliest pages.
  - MISS: an origin on a news page.

## G5. "More than 80% of the ocean is unexplored."

- **Kind:** findable origin (government wording), with an uncertain first date.
- **True origin:** NOAA's own wording, "more than 80% of our ocean is unmapped, unobserved and unexplored", on noaa.gov's ocean and coasts pages. Excerpt. A NOAA feature from 2018 says more than 80 percent. Excerpt.
- **What I couldn't pin down:** the first NOAA page and date. NOAA's "How much of the ocean have we explored?" page says it was published October 21, 2013 and last updated April 20, 2026. Its current text gives a different, newer measure: "As of April 2026, 28.7% of the global seafloor had been mapped". Checked. I couldn't reach the Wayback Machine from my tools.
- **Notes:** WHOI notes the older form was 95%, and the figure gets applied to the seafloor and to the whole ocean interchangeably.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: the earliest page is on noaa.gov, either ORIGIN_FOUND or cold.
  - PARTIAL: a noaa.gov page is among the pages but not the earliest.
  - MISS: ORIGIN_FOUND on a page that isn't NOAA's.

## G6. "Text messages have a 98% open rate."

- **Kind:** should go cold.
- **What is known:** no primary measurement exists. SMS has no read receipt for marketing messages, so an open rate can't be measured, and analysts read the 98% as a delivery rate.
  - Most pages credit Gartner, whose line was "as high as 98%" and attributed to "various sources". Gartner didn't measure it.
  - Other attributions are a 2010 Mobilesquared white paper ("98% of all SMS messages were read") and an FCC advisory committee report.
  - All of these are excerpts from vendor blogs. Gartner's original page now redirects.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: TRAIL_GOES_COLD, CONFLICTING or NO_PAGES_FOUND.
  - PARTIAL: cold, but the card sounds more certain than that.
  - MISS: ORIGIN_FOUND on any page.

## G7. "People remember 10% of what they read but 90% of what they teach others."

- **Kind:** should go cold.
- **What is known:** this is the "learning pyramid", credited to the NTL Institute (Bethel, Maine). NTL says the figures rest on its research "in the early sixties", which it can no longer find.
  - Edgar Dale's 1946 "Cone of Experience" had no percentages, and is often miscredited.
  - The percentages appear in print by 1967 (Treichler, Film and Audio-Visual Communications).
  - Sources: Wikipedia "Learning pyramid"; Training Zone, "Modern myths of learning" (2008). Excerpts.
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: not ORIGIN_FOUND.
  - MISS: ORIGIN_FOUND on any page, since the first sources are print and lost.

## G8. "It takes 10,000 hours of practice to master any skill."

- **Kind:** known misquote.
- **True origin of the number:** K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe and Clemens Tesch-Römer, "The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance", Psychological Review 100(3), 1993. The best violinists in the study had averaged about 10,000 hours of practice by age 20.
  - It was an average for one group, with wide variation. Half hadn't reached it by 20, and the figure at 18 was about 7,400 hours.
  - The study never claimed 10,000 hours makes anyone an expert. Excerpt: Ericsson's later statements as reported by Smithsonian and SCMP. I couldn't open the 1993 PDF from my tools.
- **Where the rule comes from:** Malcolm Gladwell's "Outliers" (2008, print), which Ericsson said misread the findings.
- **What changed along the way:** "the best violinists averaged about 10,000 hours by 20" became "10,000 hours to master any skill".
- **Grading:**
  - HIT: either no ORIGIN_FOUND, or ORIGIN_FOUND on the 1993 paper with a readout showing the scope widened.
  - PARTIAL: it goes cold on a page that names Ericsson or Gladwell.
  - MISS: ORIGIN_FOUND on a blog, news or book-summary page.
