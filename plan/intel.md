## 4. Rubric: official text and the weights behind it
Official (hackathon.odsc.ai config `judging[]`, VERIFIED). Each criterion has an "evidence" line: that is literally what judges are told to look for.
| Criterion | Weight | What they expect (quoted, trimmed) | Evidence judges look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sourced and verifiable answers | 30 | "citations that let a reader verify the important claims, not just a list of related links"; "Connect key factual claims to relevant, accessible sources that actually support them"; "Use timely evidence ... distinguish facts from inference"; "Identify conflicting evidence or gaps, and acknowledge uncertainty rather than inventing facts or citations" | "Show a representative answer and open its citations to demonstrate that the claims match the sources." |
| Works end to end | 25 | "real Token Factory and Tavily calls, not prewritten answers"; "Handle follow-up searches, empty results, and tool failures without silently presenting unsupported output"; "clear setup and run instructions in the README so judges can reproduce" | "Run a question ... show the resulting answer, sources, and a failure or insufficient-evidence case." |
| Usefulness | 20 | "Define the intended user and the research task"; "rather than repeating search snippets"; "Make answers and citations easy to navigate; demonstrate how the agent saves effort or improves a decision" | "Show a realistic use case, explain the user's need, and demonstrate the value" |
| Agent design | 15 | "Complexity alone is not a benefit"; "Explain query planning, tool selection, and when the agent refines its search or stops"; "how evidence is selected, deduplicated, and checked"; "Keep source content separate from trusted instructions; explain practical trade-offs around latency, cost, and reliability" | "a concise architecture overview in the README and walk through a research trace or tool-call sequence" |
| Use of Token Factory and Tavily | 10 | "how their source links survive into the final answer"; "keep API keys out of the public repository" | "redacted logs or traces without exposing credentials" |

Hidden weights (inferred, REPORTED):
- **Verification is worth about 55%, not 30%.** Criterion 1 (30) plus the failure and insufficient-evidence part of criterion 2 plus "evidence ... checked" in criterion 4 all reward the same capability. An agent that says "not enough evidence" on camera scores on three lines at once.
- **No live demo means the README and video are the whole pitch.** Judges assess "the working demo, public repo, and README" after the deadline, asynchronously, probably several judges averaged. A reproducible README (one command, `.env.example`, a sample trace) is worth more here than at an in-person event.
- **Prompt-injection hygiene is an explicit line** ("Keep source content separate from trusted instructions"). Almost nobody will show it; a 10-second demo of a poisoned page being quoted but not obeyed is cheap points.
- **Cost and latency per answer** are named in criterion 4 and are Nebius's own evaluation language ("cost per successful run"). Show a per-answer cost and time line.
- **Usefulness needs a named user.** Generic "research anything" agents lose 20 points of differentiation; a professional user with a real verification habit (signals: lawyers, clinicians, reviewers, journalists) fits best.

## 5. So what (for the planner)
1. Aim at **claim-level verification UX**: every key sentence links to the exact quoted span on the source page, with a supported / partly / unsupported / conflicting label and a published or fetched date. Opening a citation in the demo should land on the highlighted words. This is the 30% line and the generic baseline does not do it.
2. **Avoid** the Nebius cookbook clone (decompose, search, long report with numbered links), "Perplexity for X" UIs, and complexity for its own sake (multi-agent swarms without a verification story).
3. **Sponsor combo with the best odds**: Token Factory open models (a big reasoning model plus a small cheap checker, both off the Nov 9 list) + Tavily search, extract-with-query for spans, `max_age_hours` freshness, `exact_match`, and `/feedback used_citations`. Optionally confidence-gated escalation of uncertain claims (Nebius's own Tendem cookbook pattern) if a human-review step fits the user.
4. **The demo moment to engineer**: a side-by-side where a plain search-plus-LLM answer cites a page that does not say the claim, and our agent catches it (quote not found, claim marked unsupported or conflicting), then answers "insufficient evidence" honestly. Past winners won on exactly this kind of contrast.
5. **Show the numbers**: per-answer cost, latency, model IDs and a redacted tool-call trace in the README; mention the 2,231 court cases and the Lancet 12-fold rise as the why.
