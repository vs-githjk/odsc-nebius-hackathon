# Patient Zero: is there a real market need?
Owner: master-finder · goal id odsc-patient-zero-market · written 2026-10-29 00:55 PDT
Scope: Vidyut's question "how is it useful? why would people use this? is there an actual need for it in the market?", plus the conductor's 00:51 scope addition (founders and right to win, B2B / B2C / B2B2C models, real-world impact, where Patient Zero can be the best solution).

## Plain answer (read this first)
- **VERDICT: real need, narrow; weak paying demand from the people who need it most; moderate as a feature or API sold to people with budgets.** People really do trace claims back by hand (fact-checkers, journalists, Quote Investigator, Wikipedia editors, pharma reviewers). Nobody we found builds a backward family tree with an independence count. But the core users, fact-checkers, are shrinking and short of money: 437 projects worldwide and falling, 74.5% on budgets under $500k, 67.2% call themselves financially "vulnerable".
- **Best wedge:** a fact-checker or journalist checking **one viral statistic from the web era** (post-2000, online), who needs to know whether the number can run and what the true version is. That is the use with the strongest evidence and the best fit for the hackathon rubric. The money sits in adjacent places: answer engines and chatbot builders (an "origin plus independent-source count" API), content and comms teams (a per-check tool), and pharma claim substantiation (primary-source checks). That paid demand is a hypothesis: UNRESOLVED, because no buyer quote was found.
- **Biggest reason it would not be used:** the hard cases are pre-web, print or paywalled. That is where humans most need help (Quote Investigator works from book and newspaper databases), and it is where Tavily web search reaches least. Easy cases are already fast by hand ("most times you can find the first posting of a viral claim", a fact-checker in Procter et al. 2023). Patient Zero has to prove it saves time on the middle band, and a wrong "origin" damages trust in it.
- **Decision for Vidyut:** demand is good enough for the hackathon, since the rubric rewards a named user and a named decision, and fact-checkers are that user. For anything after the hackathon, reframe as the API pivot in section 5. Do not pitch it as a startup with fact-checkers as the paying customer.

## 4. Real-world impact
- **Who is helped:** readers and voters who see a corrected number; fact-checkers who get time back; journalists and analysts who avoid repeating a zombie stat (the Brexit "£90 billion" example above); policymakers, since bogus stats can end up shaping policy (the BBC reporting that statistics repeated again and again "end up influencing policy", and the coinsweekly.com piece on zombie statistics behind cultural property laws, both REPORTED via search 00:48); AI users, if answer engines call it before stating a number.
- **Scale:** fact checks reach large audiences. Google said fact-check snippets were seen 120 million times in the EU in the first half of 2024 (REPORTED, Nieman Lab via search). 62% of IFCN orgs reported audience growth in 2025 (VERIFIED). Patient Zero's direct reach would come through those orgs and through Community Notes, not on its own.
- **Who is harmed or affected:** (1) the outlet or person named as "patient zero" for a bad stat. A wrong origin is a public accusation, so every origin claim needs the two verbatim spans and an "inferred" label. (2) Original researchers, whose careful hedged finding is shown next to the viral distortion. That helps them. (3) Dual use: a mutation map shows which rewordings spread best, which a propagandist could study. Low risk, since the data come from public pages. (4) The name: "Patient Zero" comes from the misreading that blamed Gaetan Dugas (plan/audit.md finding 12). It fits the story but is a sensitivity point.
- **Do-no-harm rule for the build:** never state an origin without the verbatim span check; show "trail goes cold" instead of guessing; show what was searched.

## 5. Where Patient Zero can be the best solution, and what would make it so
**Where it can win (narrow and honest):** a statistic or short quote born on the **post-2000 web** (reports, press releases, blogs, news), repeated by dozens to thousands of pages, where the job is "how many independent sources stand behind this, where did it come from, and where did the words change". Human tracing is slow there (Charles Arthur's 2024 → 2016 → 2013 chain; the water-use chain on HN), search engines rank the reprints first, and no tool counts independent sources. Patient Zero is weakest on pre-web quotes and print-only origins (Quote Investigator's territory) and on claims already fact-checked (Full Fact claim matching, Google Fact Check Explorer, which are faster there).

**What would make it the best, not a fun project:**
1. **A published hit rate.** Run it against claims whose origins humans already traced (More or Less, Quote Investigator, Full Fact, Reason's straws figure, the Microsoft attention-span chain) with the audit's leak fix (exclude fact-check domains during the trace). Report origin found, cold trail and wrong origin rates. Nobody else publishes this.
2. **Archive reach.** Wayback CDX for dates and dead links, plus OpenAlex and Crossref for papers, so the trail does not go cold at the first scholarly hop. Print archives stay out of scope, and the product should say so.
3. **A citable output.** A one-screen "origin card" (viral wording vs. origin wording, independent-source count, links, fetch times, hashes) that a fact-checker can paste, and ClaimReview-compatible JSON.
4. **Independence count as the headline metric.** "142 articles, 1 independent source" is the number no competitor gives and the one an editor acts on.
5. **Distribution where checks already happen:** an AI Note Writer bot for X Community Notes (open API, human-rated, measurable "helpful" ratings), and an API for answer engines.

**Pivots or reframings (pick at most one after the hackathon):**
- **A. "Stat provenance API" for AI answer engines and content tools (B2B2C).** Input: a statistic. Output: origin, earliest date, independent-source count, mutation flags. Buyers have budgets and a measured failure to fix (35% false-claim repeat rate). Risk: buyer demand UNRESOLVED; needs 3 to 5 discovery calls.
- **B. "Primary source check" for claim substantiation (pharma and med comms, think tanks, consultancies).** Same engine, narrower question: does this citation lead to the primary source, and does the primary say this? It matches a documented MLR error ("citing secondary sources ... rather than primary data") in a funded market. Risk: enterprise sales and an incumbent (Veeva).
- Keep the browser extension idea for later: a crowded space (Flare, SuperX, "select text, right click to fact-check" Show HN).
